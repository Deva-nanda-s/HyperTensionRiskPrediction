Your Questions About Hypertension

High blood pressure, or hypertension, means your heart is working harder to push blood through your arteries. Over time, this can increase your risk of heart disease, stroke, and kidney problems. Regular checkups help catch it early.

Yes! Hypertension is often called the “silent killer” because many people have it without symptoms. Regular monitoring is key, especially if you have risk factors like age, family history, or lifestyle habits.

Poor diet (high salt, processed foods), inactivity, smoking, excess alcohol, stress, and being overweight are all major contributors to high blood pressure.

Absolutely! Eating fruits, vegetables, reducing salt, staying active, maintaining a healthy weight, and managing stress can significantly reduce your risk.

Adults should check at least once a year, or more often if you have risk factors like family history or lifestyle concerns.

Not always. Medications are used based on severity and risk, often alongside lifestyle changes.

Untreated hypertension can lead to heart attacks, strokes, kidney damage, vision issues, and more. Early detection is vital.
